The alphabet

Tap a letter to hear its call. Each code reads band · direction · shape.

How a call is built

Pitch band
Four bands between 2 and 5 kHz, where small songbirds sing. B1 is the lowest.
Direction
The call rises (↑) or falls (↓) across 700 Hz.
Shape
A straight sweep, or a hook that turns back at the end, like a “tsee-u”.
Length
70 ms for the 16 most common letters, 130 ms for the others, so everyday text sings faster.
Spaces
A pause between words, like a bird taking breath.
Frame
A high opening call (above 5 kHz) and a low closing trill (below 2 kHz). The listener uses the opening call to learn the tempo and pitch.
Accents
Folded to the base letter for now: é → e, ü → u, ß → ss. Digits are left out.
